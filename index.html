Ryan Lee

CS PhD Student, USC Information Sciences Institute

I'm a PhD student in Computer Science at USC's Information Sciences Institute, advised by Jonathan May. I study how large language models reason under uncertainty, and how they can learn to think harder only when a problem calls for it.

Today's language models spend the same computation on every step of their reasoning, whether that step is trivial or difficult. I work on compute-adaptive architectures that scale their computation to the difficulty of each step: thinking harder when a step is hard and less when it's easy. Most recently, I've studied looped language models, which reuse a shared set of layers to vary their effective depth: I found that sparse mixture-of-experts layers are critical to making them scale, and that looped models hold up better than standard ones when they exit early to save compute. I also helped pretrain Gecko, an efficient architecture for sequences of arbitrary length.

Before grad school, I worked in hardware design at Microsoft (Surface Slim Pen 2) and Apple (Touch ID on the M2 MacBook Air). I hold a bachelor's and master's in Mechanical Engineering from Stanford.
