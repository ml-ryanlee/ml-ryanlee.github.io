Ryan Lee

CS PhD Student, USC Information Sciences Institute

I'm a PhD student in Computer Science at USC's Information Sciences Institute, advised by Jonathan May. I study how large language models reason under uncertainty, and how they can learn to think harder only when a problem calls for it.

Today's language models spend the same computation on every step of their reasoning, whether that step is trivial or difficult. I work on compute-adaptive architectures that scale their computation to the difficulty of each step, along three axes: adaptive depth, by looping a shared set of layers; adaptive width, through sparse mixture-of-experts; and adaptive inference, which I'm exploring in ongoing work.

Most recently, bringing the first two together, I found that sparse layers are critical to scaling looped language models (NeurIPS 2026), and that looped models hold up better than standard ones when they exit early to save compute. Before that, I helped pretrain Gecko (COLM 2026), an efficient architecture for sequences of arbitrary length.

Before grad school, I worked in hardware design at Microsoft (Surface Slim Pen 2) and Apple (Touch ID on the M2 MacBook Air). I hold a bachelor's and master's in Mechanical Engineering from Stanford.
